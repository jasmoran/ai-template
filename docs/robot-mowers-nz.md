## User's lawn and requirements (stated 2026-11-17)

- About 250 m², flat (under 5°), irregular shape, in **4 sections joined by narrow gaps** (user, 2026-11-17), with an ungated path the mower has to cross. Gap widths not yet measured.
- The lawn sits several cm above the surrounding paths and concrete. The user wants grass growing over onto those trimmed.
- Several edges run right up to fences.
- One edge is a hedge the grass grows slightly under. The user wants the mower to go under it to stop grass spreading; it won't get near the trunks.
- Light tree cover in places, no trunks in the lawn.
- Boundary wire is fine. The user will install it themselves.
- No running costs beyond occasional blades, so no subscriptions.
- Rainproof is enough. Theft protection is nice to have but not worth paying extra for. Noise and safety don't matter much, since it runs during the day with nobody home.
- Must finish the 250 m² within 8 hours. Cutting width and runtime don't matter otherwise.

## Shortlist for these requirements

1. **Husqvarna 310E NERA ($3,790).** EdgeCut is a separate rear disc that runs along the boundary and works with a physical wire (confirmed on Husqvarna NZ's support page). It's the only wired model found that cuts the strip a wired mower otherwise leaves by fences and drops. Oversized (1,000 m²), but it's the cheapest model with EdgeCut. The 410XE has the same EdgeCut for $700 more and adds nothing this lawn needs.
2. **Worx Landroid M700 (~$2,500, user-checked 2026-11-17; the earlier ~$1,699 search figure was wrong).** Blade offset to one side ("cut to edge", about 4.5 cm from the edge of the body). Wired. At this price it's a poor middle option: about $1,150 more than the Gardena for the offset blade alone, and only about $1,300 less than the Husqvarna 310E. The edge result depends on how the wire is laid, and the classic manual says to keep the wire 26 cm from a step.
3. **Gardena Sileno City / Minimo 250 ($1,349–1,648).** Cheapest. Sized for exactly 250 m², which is fine given the 8-hour window. No edge feature, so plan on hand-trimming every edge.

Mapping (wired models): none of the shortlist builds a map. They mow in random lines, bounce off obstacles each time without remembering them, and find their way back to the dock by following a wire. To get through narrow passages or reach other areas, they're sent along a guide wire (Husqvarna, Gardena Sileno City and Life) or the boundary wire, to start points set in the app (Worx: start points along the wire plus its "AIA" narrow-passage feature). The Sileno Minimo does have a guide wire (confirmed in its operator's manual). CorridorCut handles passages 60 cm–1.5 m wide, boundary wires need to be at least 60 cm apart in a passage, and you set a distance for it to follow the guide wire before mowing. The manual doesn't say how many start points it supports. Wire-free models (RTK, camera or laser) do build maps and mow in straight stripes, with zones, no-go areas and paths between zones, but they cost more and some need subscriptions, so they were ruled out. For a flat 250 m² lawn, random mowing covers everything well within 8 hours.

### Mapping (wire-free) options, priced against the wired shortlist

Raised because of the 4 sections. Cheapest NZ wire-free models:
- **Ecovacs GOAT O600 RTK ($1,198–1,499).** RTK base station included; no subscription found. Ecovacs lab-tested it through a 0.7 m passage. No edge trimmer.
- **Segway Navimow i105A (~$2,198).** Consumer NZ 94. Segway NZ says the 2026 range ships with an RTK antenna, so the 4G box (1 year free, then about $55/yr) is optional; not confirmed for the i105A specifically. A Segway NZ page says passages should be at least 100 cm wide and 40 cm high, but it's unclear which models that applies to.
- Mova ViAX 250 ($1,599): **dropped.** Discontinued in Europe, 250 m² is no headroom, and a user reports it stays ~40 cm off the edges.
- Mammotion Yuka mini 2 ($2,999) and Ecovacs A2000 LiDAR Pro ($3,499, has an edge trimmer) are dearer options.
Compared with the Gardena ($1,349), mapping costs between about $0 (O600) and $850 (i105A) more. Wire-free also means no wire to lay through the gaps and across the path. RTK needs a clear view of the sky, so reception under the hedge may be patchy.

Physical limits (apply to every model):
- No robot mower trims grass hanging over a raised edge onto a lower path. Hand trimming stays, or a flush mowing strip removes the problem.
- Wire distance rules: about 35 cm from fences and walls; 10 cm from paving only if the paving is within about 1 cm of lawn level; and keep away from drops (Worx says 26 cm from a step). With a lawn several cm above the path, the path crossing needs a ramp or levelled section.
- Under the hedge: lay the wire as far under as it should mow. Low branches can set off the bump sensor.

## Extra columns suggested to the user

Slope rating and drive (2WD/AWD), navigation type and what it needs (RTK base station, network RTK/4G subscription, clear sky view under trees), narrow-passage and multi-zone handling, cut height range, obstacle/hedgehog/pet safety, rain and IP rating, noise, anti-theft (GPS/4G, PIN), ongoing costs (subscriptions, blades), NZ warranty and local service.

## Open questions

- How narrow are the gaps between the 4 sections? Wired needs at least ~60 cm between wires; the O600 was lab-tested through 0.7 m; Navimow may need ~1 m.

- Step at the path: measured by the user as ~3 cm and abrupt (2026-11-17). Too high for a wired mower to cross, since manufacturers want paving within ~1 cm of lawn level. Options: raise the path, or (cheaper) lift the turf along the path edge, take out ~3 cm of soil and relay it so it sits flush. Once flush, keep the path inside the boundary wire and the mower crosses it and cuts the path edges. Still open: does the path run between two lawn areas, along the edge of the lawn, or out of the mowing area?
- Would the user add flush mowing strips along paths and fences? That would remove most of the hand-trimming.
- Edge and area cells marked "?" need checking against spec sheets if a model makes the shortlist.
